\section{Why Matching Fails: Variance Belongs to the Reference}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\paragraph{Prediction 2: Cross-family variance decomposition.}
Decomposing model-centered \tfa{} scores across $11$--$15$ models from four families reveals that $83$--$92\%$ of per-solution variance is an identical \emph{item effect} across families (Table~\ref{tab:decomp}).
The item effect is estimated as the unweighted cross-family mean of model-centered \tfa{} scores, with $95\%$ item-bootstrap confidence intervals (widths $0.03$--$0.04$) and leave-one-model-out jackknife ranges.
Deleting any single model shifts the item share by at most $0.055$ (\textsc{GSM8K} $0.800$--$0.883$; \textsc{MATH-500} $0.887$--$0.913$), establishing that the component is a property of the benchmark reference rather than any single model family.
While difficulty and reference compressibility are entangled inside this item component, removing model and item effects leaves only $5$--$11\%$ residual variance per solution, and nothing in our data links that residual to solution soundness.
An additional $3$--$7\%$ belongs to family-specific style, growing more than twofold from \textsc{Putnam-AXIOM} ($2.9\%$) to \textsc{GSM8K} ($7.2\%$).
The same decomposition structure holds on executed code ($78\%$ item, $8\%$ family, $14\%$ residual) and in similarity metrics ($69\%$ TF-IDF, $\sim 70\%$ embeddings, $45$--$55\%$ unigram-F1).
As a pooled corollary across $6{,}000$ model--task pairs on \textsc{MATH-500}, \tfa{} correlates with reference bits-per-character at $r = -0.895$ ($80\%$ of pooled variance; Appendix~\ref{app:boundary}, Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}).

\paragraph{Causal interventions and failed repairs.}
Across benchmarks, \tfa{} model-ranking correlation co-varies with mean reference perplexity ($+0.89$) and saturation ($-0.81$).
To break observational confounding, we intervened directly on the \textsc{MATH-500} references.
Replacing each worked derivation with the single line ``The final answer is [answer]'' drops $17$-model ranking Spearman from $+0.35$ to $+0.02$ ($\Delta\rho = +0.33 \; [-0.16, +0.81]$ across models), and yields a decisive item-level paired-bootstrap drop of $\Delta\rho = +0.24 \; [+0.12, +0.33]$ across the $12$ models with per-item records ($+0.14 \to -0.10$, conditioning on fixed models).
To test whether length shrinkage alone caused this collapse, our pre-registered length-matched control (\texttt{P-L1}) replaced each worked derivation with identical content-free boilerplate matched to mean reference length plus the answer line.
Although mean \tfa{} remains high ($0.464$ across $17$ models), ranking stays collapsed at $\rho = -0.11 \; [-0.57, +0.35]$ ($17$ models), with a certified $12$-model item-bootstrap worked-minus-padded gap of $+0.41 \; [+0.35, +0.46]$ ($+0.14 \to -0.27$).
Finally, targeted repairs cannot rescue matching: up-weighting late reference tokens by $(\mathrm{pos}/n)^\gamma$ over $12$ per-token models decays ranking from $+0.13$ to $-0.37$ at last-$4$ tokens (missing our registered null call with a sign flip; Appendix~\ref{app:boundary}, Figure~\ref{fig:sweeps}), and residualizing reference perplexity reduces ranking to $-0.17$ while leaving per-task validity null (Appendix~\ref{app:verification}, Table~\ref{tab:ledger}).
