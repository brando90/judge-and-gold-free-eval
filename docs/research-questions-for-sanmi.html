Koyejo group · judge-free evaluation

Reliable judges without ground truth

One mission: build judges that stay reliable when they are optimized against and when the model they judge is smarter than them, in domains with little ground truth, and measure it. It grows from a finished result: a cheap grader ranks whole models well but cannot grade an individual answer, because 89 to 96 percent of the per-answer score is reference-style resemblance, not quality.

Five steps, in dependency order. The first is a gate: nothing else is measurable without it.

step 1 · gate

The yardstick: measuring a judge with little ground truth

How do you tell a good judge from a bad one when you have almost no labels? Split its score into a real-quality part and a surface-nuisance part (the nuisance fraction) from a small anchor. It must be a mechanism, not a trained metric, so it cannot itself be gamed. Every step below needs this number.

step 2 · smarter models

Does it survive smarter models?

Does the judge's reliability fall predictably as the model it judges grows more capable than it, and does the yardstick predict the fall? Plot reliability against the capability gap across domains.

step 3 · reward hacking

Does it survive reward hacking?

When a model is optimized against the judge, does the judge break, and can we predict which judges break before we optimize, from the nuisance fraction alone?

step 4 · design

How do we design one that survives both?

What construction holds up on both axes where ground truth is scarce: reference-free and process signals grounded in an environment oracle, ensembles, self-consistency, debate? Build candidates, score each with step 1, keep what lowers the nuisance fraction and flattens the curves.

step 5 · the floor

Where is the floor?

Is there an open-ended regime, with no checkable answer, where no cheap judge survives, so the honest design keeps a human or an environment oracle in the loop? Find where the nuisance fraction cannot be driven down. That boundary is a result in itself.

The spine

One static, mechanism-based quantity, the nuisance fraction, predicts both capability-gap collapse (step 2) and hackability (step 3). That single predictor is what makes this one program rather than five errands, and the literature pass finds it unclaimed: the closest work (Inside the Unfair Judge, arXiv:2607.11871; Singhal et al. length correlations, arXiv:2310.03716; Eisenstein et al. Helping or Herding, arXiv:2312.09244) each foreshadows only one leg, and a mechanism is much harder to game than a trained metric.

Appendix: the five as separate bets

Before the program, these were the five as independent bets. The program reorders them: yardstick = bet 2, survives smarter models = bet 1, survives hacking = bet 4, design = bet 3, the floor = bet 5. Under each: the closest prior work and where it stops short.

scaling law

As AI gets more capable than the methods we use to check it, does our ability to catch its mistakes break down in a predictable way?

Closest prior work. Engels et al. 2025 (arXiv:2504.18530) already plots oversight success against the capability gap, but on game Elo; Agent Score Difference (arXiv:2504.03731) is a single score at parity, not a curve. Our edge: the optimization-pressure-over-time axis, and real math, code, and agentic capability rather than games.

looks right vs is right

When we grade AI answers cheaply and automatically, are we measuring whether the answer looks right, or whether it is right?

Closest prior work. JudgeBench (arXiv:2410.12784) shows judges failing at objective correctness; ODIN (arXiv:2402.07319) and Length-Controlled AlpacaEval (arXiv:2404.04475) each remove one bias. None reports the surface-nuisance fraction of the score as the headline, which our decomposition does.

checking vs doing

Is telling a good answer from a bad one really easier than producing a good answer in the first place?

Closest prior work. Weaver (arXiv:2506.18203), Generator-Validator Consistency (arXiv:2310.01846), and "Can LLMs Judge Better Than They Generate?" (arXiv:2606.28050) all study the gap for correctness only. Restricting it to quality among already-correct answers appears open.

gaming the grader

If we train an AI to score well on a cheap evaluator, does that evaluator stop being trustworthy, and can we tell in advance which ones will break?

Closest prior work. Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization (arXiv:2210.10760) predicts hacking magnitude from optimization, not from a static property; Skalse et al. (arXiv:2209.13085) prove unhackability is near-impossible. Predicting hackability in advance from a measurable surface property appears unclaimed.

no right answer

For open-ended work with no clear right answer, can anything cheap judge quality, or do we always need a human?

Closest prior work. BARTScore (arXiv:2106.11520), CCS (arXiv:2212.03827), and Self-Consistency (arXiv:2203.11171) give label-free signals, but for constrained or convergent tasks. Whether any survives into open-ended, answer-free work, or whether human judgment is the floor, is unposed.
