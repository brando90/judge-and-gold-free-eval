Koyejo group · judge-free evaluation

Which question is worth going after?

The TFA study is essentially done. A cheap, reference-anchored signal ranks models well but cannot grade an individual solution, and we can prove why: 89 to 96 percent of the per-item score is reference nuisance, not competence. The signal tracks agreement with the reference's style, not reasoning quality.

Five questions follow. Each is a different bet on what the finding generalizes to. In no particular order.

scaling law

Is scalable oversight bounded by a scaling law?

Chart oversight reliability against solver capability, not accuracy at parity. In a solver-versus-overseer setup, whose method degrades slowest as the solver outgrows the overseer and is optimized against it? The output is a curve, not a single score.

generality

Do all cheap evaluators measure surface agreement, not quality?

Is the competence-versus-nuisance decomposition we found for TFA a universal property of automatic evaluation, LLM judges and similarity metrics included?

generate vs verify

Is verifying quality fundamentally easier than generating it?

Or does the generation-versus-verification gap hold only for correctness, not for quality, as our item-level null already suggests?

optimization

Can an evaluator be trusted under optimization pressure?

Which evaluation signals survive being trained against, and can we predict in advance which ones get gamed?

no reference

Can quality be measured with no reference at all?

Where no gold answer exists (open-ended writing, research, agents), is there any cheap signal that tracks quality, or is human judgment the irreducible floor?
