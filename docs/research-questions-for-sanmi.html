Koyejo group · judge-free evaluation

Which question is worth going after?

Our study is essentially done. A cheap, automatic way of scoring answers ranks whole models well but cannot grade an individual answer, and we can prove why: 89 to 96 percent of the per-answer score reflects how predictable the reference text is, not how good the answer is. The signal tracks resemblance to the reference's style, not reasoning quality.

Five questions follow. Each is a different bet on what the finding generalizes to. In no particular order.

scaling law

As AI gets more capable than the methods we use to check it, does our ability to catch its mistakes break down in a predictable way?

If we can plot that breakdown as a curve, we can tell which checking methods will still hold up as models keep improving, instead of only comparing them on the models we have now.

looks right vs is right

When we grade AI answers cheaply and automatically, are we measuring whether the answer looks right, or whether it is right?

Our result suggests cheap graders, including using one AI to judge another, mostly reward answers that resemble the reference, not answers that are actually better.

checking vs doing

Is telling a good answer from a bad one really easier than producing a good answer in the first place?

We found that judging plain correctness is easy but judging quality is not, so the common assumption that checking is easier than doing may only hold for right-or-wrong tasks.

gaming the grader

If we train an AI to score well on a cheap evaluator, does that evaluator stop being trustworthy, and can we tell in advance which ones will break?

An evaluator that looks accurate can fall apart once a model is optimized to satisfy it (reward hacking). The open question is which signals survive that pressure.

no right answer

For open-ended work with no clear right answer, can anything cheap judge quality, or do we always need a human?

Essays, research, and long agent tasks have no gold answer to compare against, so it is unknown whether any low-cost signal tracks quality at all.
