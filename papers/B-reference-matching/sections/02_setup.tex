\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models and data.}
Seventeen instruction-tuned models from four families (Qwen, Phi, OLMo, and Mistral), spanning 0.5B to 32B parameters; the full list is in Appendix~\ref{app:dnb}.
Math and edition variants (Qwen-Math, Ministral, Mathstral) are counted within their base families, and every cross-family claim in this paper uses these four families.
Eight benchmarks: \textsc{MATH-500} and full \textsc{MATH}~\cite{hendrycks2021math}, \textsc{Putnam-AXIOM} and variations~\cite{gulati2025putnam}, \textsc{Omni-MATH}, \textsc{GSM8K}~\cite{cobbe2021gsm8k}, \textsc{GSM8K-Platinum}, and \textsc{HumanEval}~\cite{chen2021codex}/\textsc{MBPP+}~\cite{austin2021mbpp,liu2023evalplus}, plus \textsc{SWE-bench Verified} as an agentic setting (patch-level \tfa{} over eight coder models, including the Qwen-Coder size ladder).
Every model generates its own solution to every problem (greedy), and is separately teacher-forced on the gold solution.

\paragraph{The comparison class.}
Five ways of scoring a solution against the reference, chosen to span the space: \tfa{} (hard argmax match on gold tokens), gold-token log-probability (the soft version), TF-IDF cosine, unigram-F1, and sentence-embedding cosine.\footnote{TF-IDF and unigram-F1 are recomputed with a self-contained implementation (IDF fit over the 500 references); values differ slightly from earlier runs, and the canonical script is authoritative.}
The first two measure the model's predictive fit to the reference text.
The last three measure how much the model's own output resembles it.

\paragraph{The verification class.}
\begingroup
\sloppy
Three judge-free signals that consume the reference only through a checkable endpoint, plus the free baseline they all must beat: the machine-checked \emph{answer-correct bit}, which costs nothing wherever the gold final answer is extractable and matchable (\texttt{papers/analysis/free\_baseline.json}).
\emph{Executed tests}: for code, run the official suite.
\emph{Prefix-repair curve}: truncate the solution at depth $k \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$, let a fixed mid-sized completer (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, eight completions per cut point; Appendix~\ref{app:dnb}) finish it, and score the fraction of completions that reach the correct final answer~\cite{lightman2023verify,wang2022selfconsistency,wang2024mathshepherd,luo2024omegaprm}.
The profile over $k$ is the signal, and $k{=}0$ is a pure problem-difficulty probe.
\emph{Answer-swap counterfactual}: the per-token log-probability margin a fixed scorer assigns the solution's own final answer over a swapped alternative, given the reasoning prefix.
LLM judges sit in either class depending on protocol; auditing judges is the subject of companion work~\cite{companionA}, and three frontier judge configurations appear here only as comparison points.
\par
\endgroup

\paragraph{Human grades.}
Annotators graded solutions blind, with no model names and no metric scores, on two 0--10 scales with written anchors: \emph{soundness} (is every step valid; ignore the writing) and \emph{style} (is it clear and well-organized; ignore correctness).
Two annotators graded a shared block of $n{=}20$ solutions to estimate cross-rater agreement: soundness Pearson $0.80$, Spearman $0.70$, and style Pearson $0.52$ (\texttt{papers/analysis/dependability.json}).
The second rater graded overall quality under an older schema rather than soundness under this paper's rubric, and clarity rather than style, so these figures are an approximate reliability estimate, not a same-rubric replication.
Because every validity correlation in this paper compares a signal to a single rater's labels, the relevant comparator is not the inter-rater Pearson $0.80$ but the implied single-rater ceiling $\sqrt{0.80} = 0.89$ (\texttt{one\_rater\_ceiling} in the same artifact).
At $n{=}20$ the inter-rater figure carries a wide interval, roughly $[0.55, 0.92]$, and the schemas differ across raters, so the ceiling itself is uncertain and every ceiling comparison in this paper is indicative only.
The primary anchor is a single uniformly random label set of 60 \textsc{MATH-500} solutions, the \emph{60-item random label set}; every human-grade correlation in this paper joins against these labels.
A frontier LLM judge (GPT-5.2, with the gold solution in context) graded the same items and serves as an expensive-reader comparison point.
The judge's Pearson correlation with human soundness is $+0.89$ $[+0.76, +0.97]$; its Spearman is $+0.43$, depressed by range restriction, because most judge scores concentrate near the top of the scale.
The same two frontier model families also graded the items as \emph{critics} (\texttt{critic\_gpt} and \texttt{critic\_opus} in Figure~\ref{fig:class}): the same frontier models under a two-scale panel rubric, with the reference in context.
Their Pearson correlations with human soundness are $+0.91$ and $+0.88$, close to the judge's $+0.89$, but the protocols separate in rank terms (Spearman $0.43$ for the referenced judge versus $0.61$ for the GPT critic) and on the within-correct margin ($-0.14$ versus $+0.17$), so validity is sensitive to the judging protocol, not only to the underlying model.
All figures come from the canonical analysis (\texttt{papers/analysis/canonical\_validity.md}).
For code correctness no human is needed: we execute the tests.

\paragraph{Statistics and pre-registration.}
Headline correlations are Spearman or Pearson with bootstrap 95\% intervals.
Grading claims control for final-answer correctness where stated, because checking the final answer is free and is not what anyone needs a grader for.
The paper designates three primary endpoints: the class-level grading null (RQ1), the item-share decomposition (RQ2), and the ranking-boundary ladder counts (RQ3); all other interval-excluding results, including individual ladder cells, intervention deltas, completer replications, and depth points, are secondary and uncorrected for multiplicity.
The four new-metric predictions of Section~\ref{sec:slate} were pre-registered: analysis scripts and directional predictions were committed to the repository before the corresponding data were generated.
