\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

This section fixes the models, benchmarks, signals, and ground truth used throughout, and defines the paper's five recurring terms: \tfa, the anchor set, reference perplexity, the completion curve, and the free bit.

\paragraph{Models and data.}
Seventeen instruction-tuned models from four families (Qwen, Phi, OLMo, and Mistral), spanning 0.5B to 32B parameters; the full list is in Appendix~\ref{app:dnb}.
Math and edition variants (Qwen-Math, Ministral, Mathstral) are counted within their base families, and every cross-family claim in this paper uses these four families.
Eight benchmarks: \textsc{MATH-500} and full \textsc{MATH}~\cite{hendrycks2021math}, \textsc{Putnam-AXIOM} and variations~\cite{gulati2025putnam}, \textsc{Omni-MATH}, \textsc{GSM8K}~\cite{cobbe2021gsm8k}, \textsc{GSM8K-Platinum}, and \textsc{HumanEval}~\cite{chen2021codex}/\textsc{MBPP+}~\cite{austin2021mbpp,liu2023evalplus}, plus \textsc{SWE-bench Verified} as an agentic setting (patch-level \tfa{} over eight coder models, including the Qwen-Coder size ladder).
Every model generates its own solution to every problem (greedy), and is separately teacher-forced on the gold solution.

\paragraph{Matching metrics.}
Five ways of scoring a solution against the reference, chosen to span the space: \tfa{} (hard argmax match on gold tokens), gold-token log-probability (the soft version of \tfa), TF-IDF cosine, unigram-F1, and sentence-embedding cosine.\footnote{TF-IDF and unigram-F1 are recomputed with a self-contained implementation (IDF fit over the 500 references); values differ slightly from earlier runs, and the canonical script is authoritative.}
The first two measure the model's predictive fit to the reference text; the last three measure how much the model's own output resembles it.
A recurring quantity on the prediction-fit side is \emph{reference perplexity}: how hard the reference text is to predict, measured as bits-per-character under the scoring model.

\paragraph{Verification signals.}
Three judge-free signals consume the reference only through a checkable endpoint, plus the free baseline they all must beat: the machine-checked \emph{answer-correct bit} (hereafter \emph{the free bit}), which costs nothing wherever the gold final answer is extractable and matchable.
\emph{Executed tests}: for code, run the official suite.
\emph{Prefix-repair completion curve} (hereafter \emph{the completion curve}): truncate the solution at depth $k \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$, let a fixed mid-sized completer (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, eight completions per cut point; Appendix~\ref{app:completer}) finish it, and score the fraction of completions that reach the correct final answer~\cite{lightman2023verify,wang2022selfconsistency,wang2024mathshepherd,luo2024omegaprm}.
The profile over $k$ is the signal, and $k{=}0$ is a pure problem-difficulty probe.
\emph{Answer-swap check}: the per-token log-probability margin a fixed scorer assigns the solution's own final answer over a swapped alternative, given the reasoning prefix.
LLM judges sit in either class depending on protocol; auditing judges is the subject of companion work~\cite{companionA}, and three frontier judge configurations appear here only as comparison points.

\paragraph{Human grades.}
Ground truth is blind human grading, plus executed tests on code, where no human is needed.
Annotators graded solutions with no model names and no metric scores, on two 0--10 scales with written anchors: \emph{soundness} (is every step valid; ignore the writing) and \emph{style} (is it clear and well-organized; ignore correctness).
The primary anchor is a single uniformly random set of 60 \textsc{MATH-500} solutions, the \emph{anchor set}; every human-grade correlation in this paper joins against these labels.
Cross-rater agreement is estimated twice, and the two estimates agree; the same-rubric estimate from a hypothesis-blind non-author annotator puts soundness agreement at $0.83$, and because every validity correlation compares a signal to a single rater's labels, the relevant comparator is the implied single-rater ceiling, about $0.91$.
Both reliability samples are small, so the ceiling is uncertain and every ceiling comparison in this paper is indicative only; the full reliability cells are in Appendix~\ref{app:ceiling} and Table~\ref{tab:robust}.
A frontier LLM judge (GPT-5.2, with the gold solution in context) graded the same items as an expensive-reader comparison point, correlating $+0.89$ with human soundness; the same two frontier model families also graded the items as \emph{critics} under a two-scale panel rubric with the reference in context.
Judge and critic Pearson values are close, but the protocols separate in rank terms and on the within-correct margin (Table~\ref{tab:classfull}), so validity is sensitive to the judging protocol, not only to the underlying model; the referenced judge's low Spearman reflects range restriction, since most judge scores concentrate near the top of the scale.

\paragraph{Statistics and pre-registration.}
Headline correlations are Spearman or Pearson with bootstrap 95\% intervals (Appendix~\ref{app:citwin}).
Grading claims control for final-answer correctness where stated, because checking the final answer is free and is not what anyone needs a grader for.
The free bit's high raw correlation with soundness is expected by construction, since unsound reasoning usually lands on a wrong answer and raters may not fully separate the two constructs even under a rubric that asks them to; we therefore treat the bit as the baseline to beat, not as a finding, and place the grading question on the within-correct margin.
The recurring analysis subsets, the 60-item anchor set, its 48-item correct-only margin, the 42-item logprob and completer-replication subset, the twelve-model per-token grid, and the nine-model answer-swap grid, are enumerated in Appendix~\ref{app:subsets} and the notes to Table~\ref{tab:citwin}.
The paper designates three primary endpoints: the class-level grading null (RQ1), the item-share decomposition (RQ2), and the ranking-boundary ladder counts (RQ3); all other interval-excluding results, including individual ladder cells, intervention deltas, completer replications, and depth points, are secondary and uncorrected for multiplicity.
Six directional predictions were pre-registered: analysis scripts and predictions were committed to the repository before the corresponding data were generated, and the complete ledger of calls and outcomes is Table~\ref{tab:ledger}.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
Every number in this paper is produced by committed analysis scripts in the released repository, which also ships the scores, labels, per-token hit vectors, decomposition outputs, labeling instruments with returned labels, and the pre-registered predictions; all anchor-set numbers come from a single canonical analysis script, and the figures regenerate from the released artifacts.
Bootstrap intervals are computed with fixed seeds committed alongside the scripts, and independently seeded runs of the same estimator can differ in the last quoted digit; where two such runs are quoted, as with the phi-4 completer interval ($[+0.68, +0.91]$ full-set, $[+0.68, +0.92]$ matched-set), each figure is labeled with its run (Appendix~\ref{app:completer}).
