\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Four lines of work border this paper: the proposal of \tfa{} itself, model-level likelihood as an ability measure, judge and reference-metric audits, and completion-based verification.

\paragraph{Teacher-forced accuracy.}
\tfa{} was introduced by \textsc{Putnam-AXIOM}~\cite{gulati2025putnam} as a judge-free evaluation signal for mathematical reasoning.
This paper does not introduce the metric; it characterizes it.
Relative to that proposal, our results are a corrective map: \tfa{} ranks models where references are worth predicting, saturates and inverts on easy benchmarks, and never grades individual solutions, because most of its per-solution variance belongs to the reference.

\paragraph{Model-level likelihood and ability.}
That model-level likelihood tracks ability is established prior art: compression of held-out text correlates linearly with benchmark performance~\cite{huang2024compression}, and likelihood-based statistics support scaling-law prediction of generative-benchmark scores~\cite{schaeffer2025pretraining} and cross-scale forecasts of reasoning performance~\cite{koh2025rbridge}.
We treat that model-level correlation as known.
The delta here is the item-level, cross-family decomposition of the same signal, the saturation boundary it predicts, and the reference interventions that probe the mechanism.

\paragraph{Judges, reference metrics, and biases.}
Reliability audits of LLM judges conclude that reliability without validity is the field's open problem, and they call for variance-component analysis of the kind performed here~\cite{zheng2023judging,tan2024judgebench}.
Cheap reference-based metrics descend from text-generation evaluation~\cite{yuan2021bartscore}, where they are typically validated against human preferences of fluency; we test them against blind soundness grades and an executable oracle, and locate their variance in the reference itself.
The style-over-substance literature documents evaluator biases in general response pools~\cite{wu2023style,dubois2024length,singhal2023long}; our contribution is complementary, a mechanism (the reference-nuisance decomposition) and a corroborating intervention (the short-reference collapse, confirmed by its registered length-matched control) for reference-anchored metrics specifically.

\paragraph{Completion-based verification.}
Completion-based process values descend from rollout estimation in process supervision~\cite{lightman2023verify,wang2022selfconsistency}, and completion-from-prefix scoring is the core of step-level verifiers such as Math-Shepherd~\cite{wang2024mathshepherd} and OmegaPRM~\cite{luo2024omegaprm}, which use Monte-Carlo rollouts from partial solutions to train process reward models.
The completion curve adapts that rollout machinery from reward-model training to judge-free scoring of full solutions against blind human soundness; its depth profile (monotone in Pearson, non-monotone in Spearman), the class-level advance-prediction test of the matching/verification taxonomy, and the free-baseline comparison showing the curve adds nothing over the free bit on our anchor set are the parts we have not seen in that line.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reference-based evaluation has been treated as a spectrum of increasingly good approximations to judgment.
It is not a spectrum.
It is two mechanisms, and in the settings we tested neither grades beyond correctness.
Signals that compare a solution to reference text measure the reference: a cross-family item share of $83$--$92\%$ in point estimates, and $80\%$ reference perplexity in the pooled corollary.
No reweighting recovers a grading signal, because there is none to recover.
Signals that verify a checkable endpoint carry real signal, but on our anchor set everything they carry is already in the endpoint itself: the free bit correlates $+0.92$ with human soundness (a baseline expected from construct overlap), the completion curve reaches $+0.79$, statistically indistinguishable from and slightly below it, and the curve's partial correlation with soundness given correctness is $-0.10$.
The space we tested is therefore a clean negative map with a mechanism: no cheap signal, matching or verification, grades beyond correctness, and correctness itself is free where references exist.
What remains open is the setting the map does not cover, solutions whose final answer cannot be extracted or matched, where completion-based verification is the natural candidate and is untested here.
The boundary for leaderboard use is equally mechanistic: matching ranks models exactly when references are worth predicting, and reference perplexity can be measured in advance to check that condition.
The answer-line intervention corroborates this reading, and its registered length-matched control (P-L1) has run and landed as predicted, excluding the variance-compression alternative; the evidentiary weight is stated precisely in Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}: the certified statement is the item-level paired delta on the twelve-model subset, which excludes zero while conditioning on the fixed models, and the seventeen-model collapse is a point contrast whose model-level delta does not (endpoints and intervals in Table~\ref{tab:robust}).
The registered record is four of six predictions landed as called, one sign flip, and one falsification (Table~\ref{tab:ledger}); the completer-strength falsification is a miss for the register and corroboration for the mechanism, since the middle-band prediction assumed graded process evaluation, which the within-correct nulls show the curve does not perform, and the class-level register did not anticipate that the free bit would top the verification class.
