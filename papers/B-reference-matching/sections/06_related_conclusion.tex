\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Teacher-forced accuracy.}
\tfa{} was introduced by \textsc{Putnam-AXIOM}~\cite{gulati2025putnam} as a judge-free evaluation signal for mathematical reasoning.
This paper does not introduce the metric; it characterizes it.
Relative to that proposal, our results are a corrective map: \tfa{} ranks models where references are worth predicting, saturates and inverts on easy benchmarks, and never grades individual solutions, because most of its per-solution variance belongs to the reference.

\paragraph{Model-level likelihood and ability.}
That model-level likelihood tracks ability is established prior art: compression of held-out text correlates linearly with benchmark performance~\cite{huang2024compression}, and likelihood-based statistics support scaling-law prediction of generative-benchmark scores~\cite{schaeffer2025pretraining} and cross-scale forecasts of reasoning performance~\cite{koh2025rbridge}.
We treat that model-level correlation as known.
The delta here is the item-level, cross-family decomposition of the same signal, the saturation boundary it predicts, and the reference interventions that probe the mechanism.

\paragraph{Judges, reference metrics, and biases.}
Reliability audits of LLM judges conclude that reliability without validity is the field's open problem, and they call for variance-component analysis of the kind performed here~\cite{zheng2023judging,tan2024judgebench}.
Cheap reference-based metrics descend from text-generation evaluation~\cite{yuan2021bartscore}, where they are typically validated against human preferences of fluency; we test them against blind soundness grades and an executable oracle, and locate their variance in the reference itself.
The style-over-substance literature documents evaluator biases in general response pools~\cite{wu2023style,dubois2024length,singhal2023long}; our contribution is complementary, a mechanism (the reference-nuisance decomposition) and an intervention (the short-reference collapse) for reference-anchored metrics specifically.

\paragraph{Completion-based verification.}
Completion-based process values descend from rollout estimation in process supervision~\cite{lightman2023verify,wang2022selfconsistency}, and completion-from-prefix scoring is the core of step-level verifiers such as Math-Shepherd~\cite{wang2024mathshepherd} and OmegaPRM~\cite{luo2024omegaprm}, which use Monte-Carlo rollouts from partial solutions to train process reward models.
The prefix-repair curve adapts that rollout machinery from reward-model training to judge-free grading of full solutions against blind human soundness; its depth profile (monotone in Pearson, non-monotone in Spearman), its difficulty-controlled reading, and the class-level advance-prediction test of the comparison/verification taxonomy are the parts we have not seen in that line.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reference-based evaluation has been treated as a spectrum of increasingly good approximations to judgment.
It is not a spectrum.
It is two mechanisms.
Signals that compare a solution to reference text measure the reference: $83$--$92\%$ cross-family item nuisance, and $80\%$ reference perplexity in the pooled corollary.
No reweighting recovers a grading signal, because there is none to recover.
Signals that verify a checkable endpoint deliver something narrower than grading: executed tests are a correctness oracle, and the prefix-repair curve is a judge-free detector of whether reasoning reaches the right answer, requiring the gold final answer, with a difficulty-controlled partial of $+0.69$ and a class-level success called by a pre-registered prediction.
The evidence that any cheap signal grades beyond correctness is absent on this anchor set, so the beyond-correctness grader remains an open problem.
The boundary for leaderboard use is equally mechanistic: matching ranks models exactly when references are worth predicting, and reference perplexity can be measured in advance to check that condition.
The answer-line intervention supports this reading with a large point contrast ($+0.35 \to +0.02$) that is significant under item-level pairing on the twelve-model subset ($+0.24$ $[+0.12, +0.33]$) and directionally consistent at all seventeen models ($+0.33$ $[-0.16, +0.81]$).
Three of the four pre-registered predictions landed as called; the position-weighted register predicted a null, and the observed sign flip to $-0.37$ at last-four tokens means that register was directionally wrong, not confirmed.
The registered record is therefore consistent with the class divide, with that one miss on record.
