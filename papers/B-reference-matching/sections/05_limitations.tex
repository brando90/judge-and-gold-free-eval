\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The human anchor is the 60-item random label set, graded by one annotator who is also an author.
A hypothesis-blind annotator external to the author list has now regraded 30 of the 60 items under the same rubric, on a blinded instrument with model names and machine scores stripped: soundness Pearson $0.83$ $[0.44, 0.97]$ and style Pearson $0.48$ $[0.14, 0.71]$, implying single-rater ceilings of about $0.91$ and $0.69$ (\texttt{papers/analysis/interrater.json}).
These same-rubric figures agree with the earlier cross-construct estimate from the $n{=}20$ shared block (soundness Pearson $0.80$, implied ceiling $0.89$; that second rater used an older overall-quality schema), which retroactively supports the older anchor.
The residual caveats are that the same-rubric replication covers 30 of the 60 items, that its intervals are wide at $n{=}30$ so every ceiling comparison in this paper remains indicative only, and that the soundness Spearman of $0.46$ reflects rank compression from ties rather than disagreement on levels.
The free-baseline comparison (\texttt{papers/analysis/free\_baseline.json}) is computed on the same 60 anchor items, so its partial-correlation nulls inherit that set's width, and the prefix-repair curve's residual use case, settings where a final answer cannot be extracted or matched, is untested here.
One registered follow-up is queued on the cluster and not yet run: a length-matched uninformative-reference control for the answer-line intervention (P-L1, registered prediction: ranking stays collapsed), which will separate information loss from variance compression in the collapse.
The other queued register, the completer-strength sweep (P-C1), has since run and falsified its middle-band prediction: the curve's validity is flat from a 1.5B to a 32B completer, and the within-correct curve mean stays null at every size (Section~\ref{sec:verify}; \texttt{papers/analysis/completer\_sweep.json}).
The original prefix-repair completer shares a model family with a subset of the graded traces; a cross-family replication with a phi-4 completer reproduces the curve's magnitude on the 42 anchor traces it covers (curve mean $+0.82$ $[+0.68, +0.91]$, versus $+0.89$ $[+0.76, +0.97]$ for the Qwen completer on the same matched traces, overlapping intervals; \texttt{papers/analysis/pv\_completer.json}).
The 18 traces the phi-4 completer does not cover are exactly the 18 phi-4-generated anchor traces, so the replication contains zero same-family traces by construction and the missingness is deterministic, not selective.
The matched 42 traces are all Qwen-generated, and sixteen were generated by the Qwen-14B completer itself; the Qwen completer's matched-subset $+0.89$ and full-set $+0.79$ differ by subset composition, and we do not attribute a cause (the excluded phi-4 traces are, if anything, easier to complete: mean process value $0.776$ versus $0.725$).
The signal remains substantially correctness-mediated under both completers: among correct-only solutions the Qwen completer's $k{=}0.75$ point is $+0.19$ $[-0.20, +0.55]$ and its curve mean $+0.07$ $[-0.21, +0.33]$ ($n{=}48$; \texttt{papers/analysis/within\_correct\_cis.json}), and the phi-4 completer's within-correct curve mean is $+0.02$ ($n{=}32$).
The beyond-correctness margin is unresolved on this anchor set: every cheap signal's within-correct value, comparison or verification, sits inside wide overlapping intervals (\tfa{} $+0.21$ $[-0.10, +0.47]$, no multiplicity correction across the battery; Table~\ref{tab:classfull}), so no cheap signal here demonstrably grades beyond correctness; the referenced judge reads $-0.14$ $[-0.25, -0.09]$ on the random set, consistent with range restriction, and $+0.81$ within-correct only on a divergence-selected stratum of the companion audit~\cite{companionA}, where selection inflates within-correct variance by construction.
All anchor-set numbers come from a single canonical analysis script (\texttt{papers/analysis/canonical\_validity.py}); earlier drafts and decks quoted values from heterogeneous scripts and estimators, and where they differ the canonical values supersede them.
Human-grade validity claims cover one math dataset plus executable code; a second labeled dataset is future work.
Pooled seventeen-model rankings have wide intervals, so we rest the ranking claims on the within-family ladders, which are individually consistent.
Those ladders have three to five models each, so their Spearman correlations are coarsely quantized, and we read signs and ladder counts rather than magnitudes.
The \textsc{SWE-bench Verified} within-family result rests on a four-model ladder and is a preliminary observation.
