\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The human anchor is the 60-item random label set, graded by one annotator who is also an author, with a shared block graded by two annotators supplying an approximate reliability ceiling (soundness Pearson $0.80$, Spearman $0.70$; the second rater used an older overall-quality schema, so the ceiling is not a same-rubric replication).
A second rater on thirty random items and an external non-author annotator are pending, and the prefix-repair headline (Pearson $+0.79$, Spearman $+0.41$) should be treated as provisional until they land.
The original prefix-repair completer shares a model family with a subset of the graded traces; a cross-family replication with a phi-4 completer reproduces the headline on the 42 anchor traces it covers (curve mean $+0.82$ $[+0.68, +0.92]$, versus $+0.89$ $[+0.76, +0.97]$ for the Qwen completer on the same matched traces, overlapping intervals).
The 18 traces the phi-4 completer does not cover are exactly the 18 phi-4-generated anchor traces, so the replication contains zero same-family traces by construction and the missingness is deterministic, not selective.
The signal remains substantially correctness-mediated under both completers: among correct-only solutions the $k{=}0.75$ point is $+0.19$ ($n{=}48$, Qwen completer) and $+0.28$ ($n{=}32$, phi-4), and the curve mean drops to $+0.07$ and $+0.02$ respectively.
The beyond-correctness margin is unresolved on this anchor set: \tfa's within-correct $+0.21$ ($n{=}48$, intervals wide and overlapping, no multiplicity correction across the battery) ties or exceeds the verification signals' within-correct values (Appendix~\ref{app:classtable}), so no cheap signal here, comparison or verification, demonstrably grades beyond correctness; the referenced judge reads $+0.81$ within-correct only on a divergence-selected stratum of the companion audit~\cite{companionA}, where selection inflates within-correct variance by construction, and is unresolved on the random set ($-0.14$).
All anchor-set numbers come from a single canonical analysis script (\texttt{papers/analysis/canonical\_validity.py}); earlier drafts and decks quoted values from heterogeneous scripts and estimators, and where they differ the canonical values supersede them.
Human-grade validity claims cover one math dataset plus executable code; a second labeled dataset is future work.
Pooled seventeen-model rankings have wide intervals, so we rest the ranking claims on the within-family ladders, which are individually consistent.
Those ladders have three to six models each, so their Spearman correlations are coarsely quantized, and we read signs and ladder counts rather than magnitudes.
The \textsc{SWE-bench Verified} within-family result rests on a four-model ladder and is a preliminary observation.
