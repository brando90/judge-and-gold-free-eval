\subsection{Grading rubric and blind protocol}
\label{app:rubric}
Soundness anchors (0--10 scale, verbatim): ``2 = major logical error or unjustified leap; 5 = partly justified, real gaps; 8 = fully valid, at most minor slips.''
Style anchors (0--10 scale, verbatim): ``2 = confusing or rambling; 5 = readable; 8 = clear and well-structured.''
Annotators also answered two binary questions per solution: \emph{answer-correct} (is the final answer correct?) and \emph{lucky-correct} (is the final answer correct even though the reasoning does not soundly support it?).
Blind protocol: annotators saw only the problem statement and the solution text, with no model names, no metric scores, and no other annotator's grades, and items were presented in randomized order.

\subsection{Prefix-repair completer}
\label{app:completer}
\begingroup
\sloppy
The fixed completer is Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct.
At each cut depth $k \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$ it generates eight completions per cut point, for thirty-two completions per solution.
A cross-family replication uses phi-4 as the completer under the same protocol; it covers 42 of the 60 anchor traces ($n{=}32$ within-correct), and its numbers ship in the unified artifact \texttt{papers/analysis/pv\_completer.json}.
The 18 uncovered traces are exactly the 18 anchor traces generated by phi-4 itself (\texttt{missing\_by\_trace\_model}: phi-4, 18), so the replication contains zero Phi-family traces by construction and the missingness is deterministic, not selective.
On the matched 42 traces the Qwen completer's curve mean is $+0.89$ $[+0.76, +0.97]$ against phi-4's $+0.82$ $[+0.68, +0.92]$, overlapping intervals.
The matched 42 traces are all Qwen-generated, and sixteen of them were generated by the Qwen2.5-14B completer itself.
The Qwen completer's matched-subset $+0.89$ and full-set $+0.79$ differ by subset composition, and we do not attribute a cause.
A direct check rules out the explanation that the excluded traces are harder to complete: the Qwen completer's mean process value is $0.776$ on the 18 excluded phi-4 traces versus $0.725$ on the 42 covered traces, so the excluded traces are, if anything, easier to complete.
The registered completer-strength sweep (P-C1) reruns the curve with Qwen2.5 completers at 1.5B, 3B, 7B, and 32B under the same protocol, on the 42 anchor traces the pairs file covers ($n{=}32$ within-correct).
Curve-mean Pearson with human soundness by size: $+0.78$ $[+0.63, +0.89]$, $+0.80$ $[+0.64, +0.91]$, $+0.88$ $[+0.73, +0.96]$, $+0.87$ $[+0.73, +0.96]$; within-correct curve means $+0.06$, $+0.12$, $+0.23$, $+0.02$; completer base solve rates $0.49$, $0.67$, $0.75$, $0.83$ (\texttt{papers/analysis/completer\_sweep.json}).
The registered middle-band prediction is falsified; Section~\ref{sec:verify} states the outcome and its reading.

\endgroup

\subsection{Reliability ceiling}
\label{app:ceiling}
\begingroup
\sloppy
Cross-rater agreement is measured twice.
A same-rubric replication comes from a hypothesis-blind annotator external to the author list, who regraded 30 random anchor items on a blinded instrument with model names and machine scores stripped: soundness Pearson $0.83$ $[0.44, 0.97]$ (Spearman $0.46$, compressed by ties) and style Pearson $0.48$ $[0.14, 0.71]$ (\texttt{papers/analysis/interrater.json}).
The original shared block of $n{=}20$ solutions graded by both annotators gives soundness Pearson $0.80$, Spearman $0.70$, and style Pearson $0.52$ (\texttt{papers/analysis/dependability.json}).
That second rater graded overall quality under an older schema rather than soundness under this paper's rubric, and clarity rather than style, so those figures approximate reliability rather than replicate the rubric, and they come from a different sample than the 60-item random label set.
The two estimates agree, which supports the older cross-construct figure as an anchor.
Because the validity correlations compare each signal to a single rater's labels, the comparator used in the main text is the implied single-rater ceiling: $\sqrt{0.83} = 0.91$ on the same-rubric estimate, against $\sqrt{0.80} = 0.89$ on the older one (\texttt{one\_rater\_ceiling} in \texttt{dependability.json}).
Both underlying intervals are wide (the same-rubric soundness Pearson spans $[0.44, 0.97]$ at $n{=}30$; the older figure spans roughly $[0.55, 0.92]$ at $n{=}20$), which propagates to the implied ceilings.
Four signals (the free answer-correct bit, the referenced judge, and both critics) exceed $0.80$ on the anchor set, which is itself evidence that the inter-rater figure is not the attainable ceiling for such comparisons.
For all these reasons the main text treats every ceiling comparison as indicative only.
\par
\endgroup
