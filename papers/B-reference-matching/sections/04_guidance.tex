\section{Practical guidance}
\label{sec:rules}

Three rules cover every use of a reference we tested.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Check the final answer before buying any deeper signal.}
Wherever the gold final answer is extractable and matchable, the free bit correlates $+0.92$ with blind human soundness on our anchor set, and no signal we tested, matching or verification, adds measurable grading information beyond it (Section~\ref{sec:verify}).
Reach for completion-based verification only where a final answer cannot be extracted or matched, and note that we have not tested that setting.
Where you do run it, the completer's strength barely matters on our anchor: the curve's validity is flat from a 1.5B to a 32B completer (Appendix~\ref{app:completer}).
\item \textbf{Never use a reference as a similarity target.}
Its value is as context for a reader or as a checkable endpoint for a verifier (Section~\ref{sec:verify}).
Any metric that scores resemblance to reference text inherits the nuisance decomposition of Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}, and every repair we or our reviewers proposed failed as the taxonomy expects, though one registered call missed in direction (Table~\ref{tab:ledger}); in particular, matching scores should never be used as per-solution rewards or filters.
On math, no matching metric clears zero against human soundness, and unigram-F1's weakly negative exclusion is suggestive only, since the five-metric battery carries no multiplicity correction (Section~\ref{sec:classnull}).
On code, the prediction-fit metrics are indistinguishable from zero against executed tests, and the output-similarity metrics carry only a weak positive signal, far below any grading standard (Section~\ref{sec:classnull}, Table~\ref{tab:robust}).
\item \textbf{For leaderboards, run a three-step check before trusting matching.}
First, compute mean reference perplexity over the benchmark's references under any strong open model; this costs one forward pass per reference.
Second, check whether the benchmark is saturated, meaning most models under comparison already score near ceiling, and whether the references are answer lines rather than worked solutions.
Third, if either condition holds, do not use matching; if neither holds, use matching only in aggregate, and report the reference-perplexity figure alongside accuracy so readers can audit the condition.
On answer-line references, matching measures style.
\end{enumerate}
