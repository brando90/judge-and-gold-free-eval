\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Evaluators.}
We audit open-weights judges (Qwen2.5 at 1.5B, 3B, 7B, 14B), each deployed blind and with the gold reference in context, plus GPT-5.2 as the frontier judge and the same frontier model prompted to reward presentation as the sabotaged positive control.
Claude Opus serves as a blind cross-family panel for channel decomposition.
The two sabotaged ladder configurations (7B, 14B) were pressure-tested first and audited afterward: their audit runs were registered pre-run, but the verdict-versus-outcome scoring happened after the pressure outcomes were known, so they enter the prediction record as confirmatory post-registration additions rather than registered-era predictions (Section~\ref{sec:audit}; Appendix~\ref{app:confusion}).
A trained reward model baseline (Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B) is audited and pressure-tested under predictions registered before its scores were generated.

\paragraph{Human labels.}
The anchor set is a uniformly random sample of \textsc{MATH-500} solutions from seventeen models, graded blind by one annotator on two 0--10 scales with written anchors: \emph{soundness} (is every step valid; ignore the writing) and \emph{style} (is it clear and well-organized; ignore correctness).
Sixty items carry labels; per-configuration joins give $n = 53$--$60$.
A divergence-selected block, where soundness and style decorrelate ($+0.82 \to +0.31$), was graded by two annotators.
Which label set carries which number is worth stating explicitly.
The headline audit partials of Table~\ref{tab:h2h} use the random 60-item anchor set, where the two axes correlate at $+0.82$.
The decorrelated divergence stratum, selected where cheap signals disagree with the judge, is used only for the within-correct check.
Conditioning on signal disagreement induces selection effects of the Berkson kind, which is why stratum numbers are confined to that check and never enter the headline partials.
Cross-rater agreement gives the measurement ceilings, from two estimates that agree.
The primary estimate is same-rubric: a hypothesis-blind annotator external to the author list regraded 30 random anchor items on a blinded instrument with model names and machine scores stripped, giving soundness Pearson $0.83$ $[0.44, 0.97]$ and style Pearson $0.48$ $[0.14, 0.71]$ (\texttt{papers/analysis/interrater.json}).
An earlier $n{=}20$ shared block gave soundness $r = 0.80$ and style $r = 0.52$ under a schema mismatch (that second rater graded an overall-quality scale under an earlier rubric), consistent with the same-rubric measurement.
The same-rubric estimates imply single-rater attenuation ceilings of about $0.91$ for soundness and $0.69$ for style: no signal can be measured as more valid than that against one rater's labels.
Every signal-versus-human number in this paper comes from one canonical analysis script over these labels; values from earlier heterogeneous scripts are superseded.

\paragraph{Judge-side probes.}
Two recur in Section~\ref{sec:audit}.
The \emph{planted-error detection} task corrupts known-correct solutions mechanically, with every corruption verified before use, so the right verdict is never in doubt.
The \emph{multi-valid-answer} tasks are RewardBench-2 items whose prompts admit several enumerated correct answers.

\paragraph{Registration.}
The categorical audit predictions (the sabotage conviction, the ladder failures, the reference flip) were registered in a draft predating the pressure results, and the multi-valid predictions were committed before that experiment's data were generated; the pressure experiment's estimators were committed before generation.
Registration here means internal git timestamps on committed drafts and code, not an external registry.
The numeric verdict rule of Appendix~\ref{app:subsample} (convict if style partial $> 0.30$; pass if soundness partial $> 0.40$ and style partial $< 0.20$) was frozen for the label-subsampling analysis, and Section~\ref{sec:pressure} applies it uniformly to all thirteen in-domain-audited configurations and reports its one false pass.
