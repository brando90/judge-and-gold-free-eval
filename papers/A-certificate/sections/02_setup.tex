\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

This section lists the judges we audit, where the human labels come from, and what was registered when.

\paragraph{Evaluators.}
We audit thirteen configurations, all shown in Table~\ref{tab:h2h}.
The open-weights ladder is Qwen2.5 at 1.5B, 3B, 7B, and 14B, each deployed two ways: \emph{blind} (question and solution only) and \emph{referenced} (gold solution in context).
GPT-5.2 is the frontier judge, deployed honestly and, as the positive control, prompted to reward presentation (\emph{sabotaged}).
The same sabotage rubric was also run on the 7B and 14B ladder judges; those two audits were registered before running but scored against pressure outcomes already known, so they enter the record only as late-scored confirmatory additions (ledger, Table~\ref{tab:ledger}).
A trained reward model (Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B) is audited and pressure-tested under predictions registered before its scores were generated.
Claude Opus serves as a blind cross-family panel for channel decomposition.

\paragraph{Human labels.}
The anchor set is a uniformly random sample of \textsc{MATH-500} solutions from seventeen models, graded blind by one annotator on two 0--10 scales with written anchors: \emph{soundness} (is every step valid; ignore the writing) and \emph{style} (is it clear and well-organized; ignore correctness).
Sixty items carry labels; per-configuration joins give $n = 53$--$60$.
The headline partials of Table~\ref{tab:h2h} use this random anchor set, where the two axes correlate at $+0.82$.
A separate divergence-selected block, where the axes decorrelate ($+0.82 \to +0.31$), is used \emph{only} for the within-correct check: it is selected where cheap signals disagree with the judge, which induces Berkson-style selection effects, so its numbers never enter the headline partials.
This exclusion is conservative rather than selective: the block is constructed by conditioning on the judges' own signals, so admitting it to the headline partials would let selection effects contaminate the very correlations the verdict reads, while the uniformly random anchor set cannot be tuned toward any outcome.
The primary annotator is an author, so the labels were independently checked: a hypothesis-blind annotator external to the author list regraded 30 random anchor items on a blinded instrument, and on that annotator's labels alone the honest frontier pass and both headline convictions reproduce in sign (Section~\ref{sec:audit}; Table~\ref{tab:robust}).
The same replication bounds the measurement: soundness reliability $0.83$ and style reliability $0.48$ imply single-rater ceilings of about $0.91$ and $0.69$, and no signal can be measured as more valid than that against one rater's labels.
An earlier 20-item block under a different rubric agrees; both estimates, with intervals, are in Table~\ref{tab:robust}.

\paragraph{Judge-side probes.}
Two mechanical probes recur later.
\emph{Planted-error detection} corrupts known-correct solutions mechanically, every corruption verified before use, so the right verdict is never in doubt.
The \emph{multi-valid-answer} tasks are RewardBench-2 items whose prompts admit several enumerated correct answers.

\paragraph{Registration.}
Every prediction, when it was registered, and how it came out is consolidated in one prediction ledger (Table~\ref{tab:ledger}); the prose elsewhere states only headline records and points there.
Registration means internal git timestamps on committed drafts and code, not an external registry; commit hashes appear in the ledger rows.
Internal timestamps establish ordering but cannot rule out selective disclosure the way an external registry would; we treat this as a limitation, and the ledger therefore reports every registered prediction including the falsified ones.
Going forward, the pending experiments carry external OSF registrations with third-party timestamps (the DPO rerun at \texttt{osf.io/jg4ak}, registered before its evaluation was read; the Putnam replication at \texttt{osf.io/axp3s}, registered before any labels were collected), while the original registered-era predictions remain internal-timestamp only.
The frozen numeric verdict rule is: \emph{convict} if the style partial exceeds $0.30$; \emph{pass} if the soundness partial exceeds $0.40$ and the style partial is below $0.20$; \emph{fail} otherwise (Appendix~\ref{app:subsample}).
Section~\ref{sec:pressure} applies it uniformly to all thirteen configurations and reports its one false pass.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
Every number in this paper is produced by a committed analysis script in the released repository; a single canonical script computes all signal-versus-human values, superseding earlier heterogeneous scripts.
The appendix notes, per table, which repository analysis produces it; commit hashes for registered predictions appear only in the ledger.
