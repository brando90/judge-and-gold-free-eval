\section{From an instrument to a procedure}
\label{sec:procedure}

This section generalizes the one audit we ran into a four-tier procedure, and reports how much of each tier this paper's data already validates.
The audit of Section~\ref{sec:audit} is one instantiation: soundness and style as axes, one dataset, one label budget.

\paragraph{Tier 0: self-consistency screens, at zero labels.}
Free checks come first, because a judge that cannot agree with itself cannot be audited.
The order-swap flip rate (Section~\ref{sec:audit}) falls from $71$--$95\%$ at 0.5B to $2$--$37\%$ at 14B, and a score-range check catches unauditable regimes (on saturated data $96\%$ of the frontier judge's scores are identical, so no validity question can be asked).
Both screens are free and gate everything downstream.

\paragraph{Tier 1: causal axis discovery, at zero human labels.}
The axes a judge should ignore can be discovered by perturbation rather than hand-picked, and this tier is now validated end to end.
Discovery must be causal rather than by regression: an observational length effect in the frontier judge's scores did not reproduce when we causally padded solutions holding content fixed, while the same causal probes measured a real first-position bias in one frontier judge (order-swap preference $0.62$) and none in another ($0.47$).
We therefore registered a fixed six-axis perturbation battery adapted from published evaluator-bias taxonomies~\cite{cobbler2023,judgebiasbench2026}: length padding, reformatting, authority cues, bandwagon cues, confident-tone injection, and hedging, each preserving mathematical content and the final answer; fixing the taxonomy before data is what blocks discovering whichever axes make the audit look good.
All ten arms (two frontier, eight ladder) have now run; per-axis score deltas with intervals are in Table~\ref{tab:battery}, and the four registered battery predictions resolve as follows (ledger, Table~\ref{tab:ledger}).
The honest frontier judge is insensitive on all six axes, confirming the registered null (P-B2).
The registered sign prediction for the sabotaged judge (P-B1) is falsified in direction: it is significantly sensitive on three axes but negatively --- appended social cues and hedges \emph{lower} its score --- so it punishes any appended imperfection rather than rewarding authority; that is still a presentation sensitivity the honest judge lacks, but the registered sign was wrong, and we report it as promised.
The blind 1.5B is positively sensitive as predicted (P-B3; primary read $+0.65$), presentation sensitivity declines with judge scale, and referenced arms are flatter than blind at each size, with one exception --- the referenced 1.5B, whose deltas are the largest of the referenced tier, consistent with its boundary status in the audit.
The tier-to-tier validation (P-B4) meets its registered criterion: across the ten configurations with both a battery arm and an audited style partial, battery sensitivity rank-correlates with the style partials at Spearman $0.55$ against a registered bar of $0.5$ --- a pass of the registered test, not evidence of a strong effect, since the margin is thin and ten configurations give a rank correlation little precision.
P-B1's falsification remains the caution: the register's sign intuitions can be wrong even when the instrument works.

\paragraph{Tier 2: target-axis validation, with an oracle where one exists.}
The target axis does not require human labels when the domain has an oracle, but the deception side still does.
Code supplies an oracle, and the mechanical-target audit of Section~\ref{sec:pressure} is the demonstration: executed tests as the target axis measured the capability gate in-domain, separated the frontier tier, and detected sabotage, all without an annotator --- while predicting inflation resistance only through the reference protocol, so human-labeled style axes remain necessary for the deception side.
Where no oracle exists, humans grade the target axis, and the divergence-subsample construction (sample where cheap signals disagree) supplies power that random sampling cannot.

A fully mechanical instantiation was also run after the human audit, with expectations stated in the analysis header before execution: machine correctness as the target axis, log response length as the ignored-property axis, zero human labels, same thirteen configurations.
Under a simple pre-stated rule --- flag a configuration if its length-partial interval excludes zero --- six of thirteen are flagged (per-configuration values in Table~\ref{tab:mech}).
The honest summary is mixed.
The zero-label audit separates the two most corrupted evaluators by margin: the sabotaged frontier and the reward model carry the two largest length-partial magnitudes ($-0.74$ and $-0.54$) with collapsed correctness partials, and the honest frontier is clean.
But the rule also flags four other configurations, including two honest referenced passes, so the stated expectation that honest judges come out clean (E1) is partially violated, and the other expectation (E2) was unfalsifiable as phrased, which is not pre-specification (ledger).
The mechanical axes are a coarse screen that orders corruption severity, not a verdict instrument; the human-labeled audit remains the verdict tier.
The length signal does feed back into the human audit as a confound check: the three-variable partials of Section~\ref{sec:audit} show both style convictions survive controlling for length (Table~\ref{tab:robust}).

\paragraph{Tier 3: the pressure test as the axis-agnostic backstop.}
Any axis missing from Tiers 1--2 can still be exploited, so the best-of-$n$ experiment is the detector of last resort, run per domain.

\paragraph{Label economics, in place of a magic number.}
The label budget is verdict-dependent, not fixed.
Empirically, convictions stabilize by roughly 40 labels while boundary passes do not (Figure~\ref{fig:stability}); analytically, cross-rater reliability bounds what labels can measure (single-rater ceilings about $0.91$ for soundness and $0.69$ for style; Table~\ref{tab:robust}), and Spearman--Brown projections say how much a second rater buys ($0.83 \to 0.91$ for soundness).
Both reliability estimates carry wide uncertainty at these sample sizes and are indicative anchors, not walls.
A registered prospective test applies these curves to predict the label budget for a second math dataset before its labels arrive (ledger).

\paragraph{What remains non-generic.}
Three limits are inherent rather than incidental.
Synthetic perturbations can be off-manifold, so Tier-1 insensitivity to an injected cue does not preclude sensitivity to its natural counterpart; Tier-1 causal sensitivity and Tier-2 in-distribution loading catch different failures and neither substitutes for the other; and outcome validation costs compute in every new domain.
The procedure narrows the unmeasured space at each tier; it does not close it.
