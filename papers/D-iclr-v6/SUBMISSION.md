Deadlines (ICLR 2027 author guidelines): abstract registration **Sep 18, 2026 AOE**; full paper **Sep 25, 2026 AOE**. Supplementary material follows the full-paper deadline; check OpenReview for the exact supplementary cutoff.

## Metadata to paste into OpenReview

- **Title:** see `main.tex` `\title` (keep identical in OpenReview).
- **Abstract:** paste the plain-text version of the `abstract` environment (replace `\%` with `%`, `$n$` with n).
- **Title (v5):** Reference Anchoring Biases LLM Judges to Match Answers Instead of Grading Them
  - Superseded (v5, earlier): Reference Anchoring Biases LLM Judges Toward the Answer They Are Shown When Several Are Correct. The new title names the mechanism (matching in place of grading) rather than the direction of the pull. Note it drops the explicit "when several are correct" scope, which the previous retitle had added on purpose; P2 shows the reference *helps* on single-answer questions (+28.6 pp on MATH-500), so if a reviewer reads the title as an unconditional claim, \cref{sec:p2} is the answer.
  - Superseded (v3, round 18): The Reference Is Not an Answer Key: LLM Judges Favor the Correct Answer They Are Shown. Retitled in v5 because the v3 title asserted unconditionally what P2 refutes on single-answer questions, where the reference helps.
- **TL;DR (v5):** When a question has several correct answers, LLM judges favor the one given as the reference: swapping it moves the score of identical text by about 3 to 5 points out of 10, correct answers that differ from it are rejected even when the judge accepts them without a reference, and benchmark graders run as shipped, with the reference drawn at random, score four models 30 to 67 points below their answer-list accuracy; choosing the reference rather than drawing it leaves two of those graders 9 to 56 points short. With one correct answer the reference helps instead.
- **Keywords:** LLM-as-a-judge, reference-guided evaluation, multiple correct answers, anchoring, best-of-n selection, reasoning judges, evaluation reliability, preregistration
- **Primary area (suggested):** alignment, fairness, safety, privacy, and societal considerations — alternative: datasets and benchmarks / evaluation. Pick whichever area list ICLR 2027 shows that mentions evaluation of foundation models.
